# Liv scoring methodology: skincare pilot

Scores are dimension-specific and independent. Liv does not compute an overall product score.

## Current pilot status

The pilot source set contains community-submitted product records and a limited number of linked manufacturer/research pages. Open Beauty Facts explicitly does not guarantee product-record accuracy. Therefore no product receives a 1–100 score or a 1–5 community rating in this pilot. The database stores each dimension as unavailable with a reason; this is intentional, not a zero score.

## Source review and exact-variant findings (2026-10-28)

The pilot checked public sources without connecting a commercial data provider or opening a paid account:

- **Open Beauty Facts:** useful for discovery and contributor label records, but the data is community-entered, may be incomplete or inaccurate, and is not a substitute for confirming the package. Its database is ODbL 1.0 and individual records are DbCL; public reuse requires attribution, and adapted databases/works can carry share-alike obligations. Keep the clickable record and clear attribution in the UI. Do not reuse product photos without checking their separate CC BY-SA and other rights. Sources: [data reuse terms](https://world.openbeautyfacts.org/data), [terms of use](https://world.openbeautyfacts.org/terms-of-use).
- **Manufacturer pages:** suitable primary references for current brand-published ingredient lists and claims, but the CeraVe page explicitly says package ingredients can change. It does not establish that its page matches the pilot’s 453 g barcode. The current First Aid Beauty page describes a 0.5% colloidal-oatmeal skin protectant and a current ingredient list; that list differs from the community record. It does not identify the pilot barcode. Link to pages and summarize findings; do not copy their photos or assume permission for bulk republication.
- **DailyMed / NLM label:** free official access to manufacturer-submitted OTC labeling. Its First Aid Beauty record identifies a 170.1 g jar, 0.5% colloidal oatmeal, multiple NDC packages, and a label update date of 2025-12-08. It is not an FDA efficacy endorsement and the NDCs have not been matched to the pilot’s barcode. Source: [DailyMed label](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=beb22d69-8a83-65dc-e053-2a95a90ad812).
- **PubMed:** free citation/abstract access; the linked colloidal-oatmeal paper reports two single-center, single-arm studies of a 1% cream. It is ingredient-level evidence, not a study of the 0.5% First Aid Beauty product. Source: [PubMed PMID 28697218](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28697218/).
- **Cost and provider decision:** no provider was connected, no account or paid access was activated, and no cost was incurred. A commercial label/review provider is not justified until its exact-variant coverage, contractual reuse rights, update cadence, attribution requirements, API limits, and price are independently reviewed and approved.

The present public evidence does **not** confirm either community barcode against a current exact-package ingredient label. CeraVe’s current manufacturer page supplies a current product reference but not the 453 g barcode/package match. First Aid Beauty’s current brand page and DailyMed label give useful current reference information, but neither supplies a verified mapping from the community barcode to the displayed package. The community First Aid Beauty ingredient transcription also conflicts with the current manufacturer list, so it must not be silently replaced or treated as a verified label.

Accordingly, all five product dimensions remain unavailable for both records. No numeric score can be published from these sources. Any later score must include a non-empty citation list, its published dimension-specific method, coverage, confidence, and review date; absence or mismatch of exact-package evidence blocks scoring.

## Eligibility and intended methods

- **Ingredients & Safety (1–100):** Do not create a universal safety score. A future score needs verified, current, exact-variant ingredient labeling, product-specific regulatory context, a disclosed hazard/evidence method, and explicit coverage/uncertainty. Ingredient presence alone is not a safety conclusion.
- **Effectiveness (1–100):** Requires evidence about the finished product and intended outcome. Ingredient-level studies are shown separately and cannot be treated as proof that a product works.
- **Transparency (1–100):** Intended to measure how completely a manufacturer discloses traceable product facts, not whether a product is good or safe. A future method should score a fixed published checklist of exact variant, full ingredient label, package size, claim substantiation, and current primary-source links. Community-submitted fields alone do not qualify.
- **Community Experience (1–100):** Requires a sufficiently sized, authentic, lawful review corpus with counts and date range. It remains separate from the star average.
- **Value (1–100):** Requires current, comparable, region-specific price and package size from a permitted source. It is not calculated from a single unverified price.
- **Community rating (1–5 stars):** Show only an authentic, permitted review source or Liv member reviews with count and source/timeframe. No reviews means no rating.

Every future score must expose its method, evidence links, coverage, confidence, and update date. Stale, conflicting, ambiguous, or missing evidence blocks a score rather than lowering it by assumption. Never imply universal safety or a personal health guarantee.
